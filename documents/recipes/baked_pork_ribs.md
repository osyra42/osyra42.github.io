# Baked Pork Ribs

**Ready to serve in:** 3 hrs 15 min

**Prep:** 15 min | **Cook:** 3 hrs | **Yields:** 4 servings

## Ingredients

- [ ] **2–2½ lbs** baby back pork ribs
- [ ] **1 tbsp** olive oil
- [ ] **¼ cup** finely diced onion
- [ ] **½ tsp** ground cumin
- [ ] **½ cup** ketchup
- [ ] **1 tbsp** hot chili sauce (like Sriracha)
- [ ] **2 tbsp** light brown sugar
- [ ] **1 tbsp** apple cider vinegar
- [ ] Salt and ground pepper, to taste

## Instructions

### 1. Prep the oven and pan
Preheat your oven to **275°F (135°C)**. Set aside a large roasting pan or rimmed baking sheet and aluminum foil.

### 2. Season the ribs
If your ribs still have a thin membrane on the back, remove it (see Notes). Generously season both sides of the ribs with salt and pepper. If using a spice rub, apply it evenly.

### 3. Wrap with foil
Place the ribs **meatiest-side down** in your roasting pan. You may need to cut the ribs in half to fit. Cover the pan or baking sheet tightly with aluminum foil. The ribs must be well-sealed to prevent drying out. If your foil isn't large enough, wrap the racks of ribs individually in tight foil packets and place them on the baking sheet.

### 4. Bake
Bake for **2½–3½ hrs**, or until the meat easily falls from the bones. Check the ribs after **2 hrs** to see how they're doing. Ribs are ready when the meat is cooked through and very tender.

### 5. Check for doneness
Pierce them with a sharp knife — there should be little to no resistance. You can also gently bend the ribs; if the meat looks like it will easily pull away from the bone, they're ready. If the meat is still tough, they're likely underbaked (your oven might run cooler). Continue baking and consider increasing the temperature to **300°F (150°C)**.

### 6. Make the BBQ sauce
While the ribs are baking, prepare your sauce. Heat the olive oil in a saucepan over **medium heat**, then add the onions and cook until soft and translucent, about **5–8 min**. Stir in the cumin and cook for another **30 seconds**. Next, add the ketchup, hot chili sauce, brown sugar, and apple cider vinegar. Stir to combine, season with salt, and cook for **2 min**. Set aside.

### 7. Brush with sauce
Remove the ribs from the oven, discard the aluminum foil, and generously brush both sides with barbecue sauce.

### 8. Broil
Move an oven rack near the top of the oven and turn your broiler to **high**. Broil the ribs until the BBQ sauce begins to caramelize, about **3–4 min**. Keep a close eye on them so the sauce doesn't burn.

## Serving Suggestions

- **For you:** Serve with a big salad (honey mustard, blue cheese, or ranch dressing all work well) for a lower-carb plate.
- **For the family:** Serve with potato salad, creamy coleslaw, corn salad, cornbread, macaroni salad, or roasted/mashed potatoes.

## Storage Tips

- Store baked ribs in an airtight container in the fridge for **up to 4 days**, or freeze for **up to 3 months** (double-wrap before freezing). Thaw frozen ribs overnight in the refrigerator.
- **Reheating:** Place the thawed ribs, wrapped in foil, in a **275°F** oven for **20–30 min**, or on the grill over **medium heat**. Slather with BBQ sauce before serving, and broil or grill to caramelize if desired.

## Notes

- **Rib varieties:** Baby back ribs are called for, but spare ribs, country-style, and St. Louis-style all work. Cook time should be similar — check on them as they cook.
- **Removing the membrane:** Place the ribs meat-side-down on a cutting board. Locate the thin membrane covering the rack, then use a knife to gently slide underneath it. Pull the membrane away using your fingers. If it's slippery, use a kitchen towel to grip and pull it off.
- **Why meatiest-side down:** The rendered fat and juices from the meat above drip down, creating a natural basting effect that keeps the meat moist during long, slow cooking.
- **Multiple racks:** Cooking time stays the same. Wrap each rack tightly in foil and place on a large baking sheet — two racks fit per sheet, so four racks can bake at once. If using two oven shelves, swap the pans halfway through to ensure even cooking.
- **Cooking on the grill:** Set up your grill for indirect heat, maintain **275°F**, and follow the same instructions as the oven method.
- **Make ahead for a crowd:** Bake the ribs ahead, then reheat gently wrapped in foil (add a splash of water or broth). Once hot, uncover, brush with sauce, and broil for a few minutes until the sauce sets. They won't dry out.
- **Ketchup matters:** The BBQ sauce is best with thicker ketchup that contains no high-fructose corn syrup. Hunt's 100% Natural or Heinz Simply work well.
- **Foil alternatives:** A roasting pan or large Dutch oven with a tight-fitting lid works — you may need to cut the racks down to fit. If you prefer foil not to touch the food, wrap in parchment first, then foil over the outside.
- **Spice rub option:** Instead of just salt and pepper, use your favorite BBQ spice rub or seasoned salt.
- **Low-carb option:** Skip the BBQ sauce and use a sugar-free rib rub instead — the ribs turn out great.
- **Reader twist:** One reader dry-rubs after a mustard binder, adds ½" apple juice to the pan, bakes 3 hrs at 275°F, then finishes with Sweet Baby Ray's under the broiler for 3 min.
