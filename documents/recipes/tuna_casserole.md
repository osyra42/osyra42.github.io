# Tuna Casserole

**Ready to serve in:** 33 min

**Prep:** 15 min | **Cook:** 18 min | **Yields:** 6 servings

## Ingredients

### For the Casserole

- [ ] **3 cups** egg noodles, measured dry (approx. **6 oz**)
- [ ] **1 tbsp** butter
- [ ] **1 small** onion, diced
- [ ] **2 ribs** celery, diced
- [ ] **⅔ cup** frozen peas, thawed
- [ ] **1 (5-oz) can** tuna, drained
- [ ] **1 (10.5-oz) can** condensed cream of mushroom soup
- [ ] **⅓ cup** milk
- [ ] **1 cup** shredded cheddar cheese
- [ ] **1 tbsp** chopped fresh parsley (or **1 tsp** dried)

### For the Crumb Topping

- [ ] **½ cup** panko bread crumbs
- [ ] **1 tbsp** melted butter
- [ ] **½ cup** shredded cheddar cheese
- [ ] **1 tbsp** chopped fresh parsley (or **1 tsp** dried)

## Instructions

### 1. Prep the topping
Preheat oven to **425°F**. Combine the topping ingredients in a small bowl and set aside.

### 2. Cook the noodles
Boil noodles **al dente** according to package directions. Drain and rinse under cold water.

### 3. Sauté the aromatics
Cook onion and celery in butter until tender, about **5–7 min**.

### 4. Combine
In a large bowl, combine the noodles, onion mixture, peas, soup, milk, cheese, tuna, and parsley. Mix well.

### 5. Assemble
Spread into a 2-qt casserole dish and top with the crumb topping.

### 6. Bake
Bake for **18–20 min**, or until bubbly.

## Serving Suggestions

- **For you:** Serve with a simple side salad or steamed green beans for a balanced plate.
- **For the family:** Serve with homemade buttermilk biscuits or a Caesar salad to round out the meal.

## Storage Tips

- Refrigerate leftovers in an airtight container for **up to 3 days**.
- **To freeze (baked):** Cool completely, wrap, and seal. Freeze for **up to 6 months**. Thaw overnight in the fridge before reheating.
- **To freeze (unbaked):** Assemble in a foil-lined pan, wrap and seal well, then freeze. Thaw fully overnight in the fridge before baking — this prevents uneven cooking. Bake until it reaches **165°F**.
- Tip: freeze in a foil-lined pan so you can lift the casserole out and keep the dish.

## Notes

- **Noodle measurement:** **1 cup** of dry egg noodles weighs about **2 oz**, so 3 cups ≈ 6 oz. Cook them **al dente** — they'll keep cooking in the oven, and you want to avoid mushy noodles.
- **Noodle swap:** Any pasta works. Note that narrower noodles can make the casserole drier — wider egg noodles hold up better.
- **Make ahead:** This casserole can be assembled ahead and baked later. Great for prepping now, baking later.
- **Veggie swaps:** Great with leftover green beans, roasted carrots, steamed broccoli, or frozen mixed veggies. If you're out of peas, just leave them out.
- **Shortcuts:** No time to sauté onions and celery? Use onion powder instead. Out of panko? Crushed potato chips or crushed Ritz crackers work well.
- **More protein:** Two cans of tuna works fine — you may want to add a touch more milk.
- **Cream of chicken swap:** Using cream of chicken instead of mushroom cuts the fishy taste from the tuna.
- **Homemade cream of soup:** Make a butter + flour roux, then add cream and chicken broth for a cheaper, tastier alternative to canned soup.
- **Cheese:** A brick of cheddar shredded fresh melts a little better than pre-shredded, but either works.
- **Bake temperature note:** One reviewer accidentally baked at 350°F for 17 min, then bumped to 425°F for 8–10 min and got great golden crumbs. If your casserole needs more time, this is a safe path.
- **Rice version:** The site tested a tuna rice casserole variation using rice in place of the noodles.
