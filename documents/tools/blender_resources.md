# 🧊 Blender Resources
::toc::
🎯 The fastest way to get good at Blender is to **finish a project.** Pick something - a coffee mug, a low-poly room, a character bust - and follow it through to a render. Everything you need to learn comes up along the way.

Here are the resources I lean on, plus the ones everyone learning Blender ends up pointed at. 🚀

🌐 **[blender.org](https://www.blender.org/)** - the official site. Free, open source, runs on Windows, Mac, and Linux.

📖 **[Blender Manual](https://docs.blender.org/manual/en/latest/)** - the reference docs. Searchable, indexed, kept current with each release.

> ℹ️ I'm on **Blender 5.1.1** at the time of writing. Most tutorials below were made on earlier versions - the fundamentals translate, but UI labels and shortcut placements may have shifted.

---

## 🍩 Start Here

The donut tutorial is the starting point everyone points to - by the time you finish it you've touched modeling, materials, lighting, and rendering.

- [Blender Guru: Donut Tutorial](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=z-Xl9tGqH14) - Andrew Price's beginner series
- [Grant Abbitt: Blender for Beginners](https://www.youtube.com/@grantabbitt) - friendly, methodical, project-based

## 🌍 Asset Libraries

- [Polyhaven](https://polyhaven.com/) - free HDRIs, textures, and models (CC0)
- [BlenderKit](https://www.blenderkit.com/) - free and paid asset library, integrates inside Blender
- [Sketchfab](https://sketchfab.com/) - large community model library
- [Blender Studio](https://studio.blender.org/) - production assets and open-movie files from the Blender Foundation
