# Mongolian Pork

**Ready to serve in:** 30 min

**Prep:** 20 min | **Cook:** 10 min | **Yields:** 4 servings

## Ingredients

- [ ] **1 lb** pork tenderloin
- [ ] **¼ cup** cornstarch
- [ ] **2 tbsp** vegetable oil
- [ ] **1 tbsp** garlic cloves, minced
- [ ] **1 tbsp** freshly grated ginger
- [ ] **½ cup** soy sauce
- [ ] **½ cup** dark brown sugar
- [ ] **¼ cup** water
- [ ] **2** green onions, chopped
- [ ] **1 tbsp** toasted sesame seeds

## Instructions

### 1. Slice the pork
Place the tenderloin in the freezer for **10 min** so it's easier to thinly slice. Slice it about **⅛"** thick if possible.

### 2. Coat the pork
Toss the pork in a bowl with the cornstarch, making sure everything is coated.

### 3. Sear the pork
Add oil to a large skillet over **medium-high heat**. Shake off any excess cornstarch from the pork and cook for **4–5 min**, or until the pork is charred. When done, remove from the skillet.

### 4. Make the sauce
Add the garlic and ginger to the skillet, cooking for **30 seconds**. Add the soy sauce, water, and dark brown sugar, and let it come to a boil.

### 5. Finish
Add the pork back in and cook for **30 seconds**. Add the green onions and serve over some rice.

## Serving Suggestions

- **For you:** Serve over cauliflower rice or steamed broccoli for a lower-carb plate.
- **For the family:** Serve over white rice, with extra green onions and sesame seeds on top.

## Storage Tips

- Refrigerate leftovers in an airtight container for **up to 3 days**.
- Reheat in a skillet over **medium-low heat** until warmed through. Add a dash of water if needed.
- **Freezer:** Leftovers freeze well for **up to 3 months**.

## Notes

- **Best pork cut:** Pork tenderloin is ideal — it's easy to slice and comes in convenient 1 lb portions.
- **Freeze before slicing:** **10–30 min** in the freezer firms the meat up so you can slice it thinly and evenly.
- **Consistency matters:** Slice the pork to a consistent size so it cooks evenly.
- **Cook in batches:** Don't overload the skillet or wok — adding too much at once cools the pan and slows the cooking. You want the pork to cook quickly.
- **Add heat (optional):** Not traditional, but if you like spice, add crushed red pepper flakes **¼ tsp** at a time.
- **Ingredient swaps:**
  - **Protein:** Flank steak or chicken work in place of pork.
  - **Thickener:** All-purpose flour can replace the cornstarch.
  - **Sugar:** Light brown sugar or granulated sugar works in place of dark brown (dark gives a deeper, more molasses-like sauce).
- **Slow cooker version:** Use pork shoulder instead of tenderloin, still sliced. Cover and cook on **HIGH for 2–3 hrs** or **LOW for 4–5 hrs**.
- **Reader additions:** Sliced onion, mushrooms, green beans, broccoli, or sliced jalapeños all work well added with the pork. One reader made the sauce on the side so people could add it to taste.
- **Equipment note:** A wok or flat-top griddle works even better than a skillet if you have one — you can cook more pork at once.
