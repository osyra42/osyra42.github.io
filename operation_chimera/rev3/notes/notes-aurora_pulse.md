# Aurora Pulse

## Current

Uta Yuki’s nightclub: a place where young adults go to dance, drink, eat, and hang out. Uta Yuki is the owner and occasional DJ.

## History

The building was originally a factory warehouse, decommissioned years before Uta picked it up. Its size and industrial bones made the conversion easy—the wide-open floor became the dance area, the side rooms became the games room and kitchen, and the high ceilings had no trouble accommodating hanging speaker poles and camera rigs.

Aurora Pulse is not where Uta started. In an earlier era, she tended bar at a smaller dance club—the same era she first crossed paths with a homeless Osyra and took her in. See **Uta Yuki**. Traces of that time carry over here, including the **Shattered Cue Ball** mocktail on the menu.

## Layout

### Dance Floor

The main room runs on black lights and neon colors. Random shapes printed across the carpet seem to glow under the blacklight. A seating ledge runs around the perimeter so people can sit against the wall—but its line is neither straight nor consistent; it bends and warps.

None of the lines in the room are straight. Combined with the chromatic effects, simply walking across the dance floor can make someone feel drunk.

### Stage

The front of the room is set up for karaoke, with the DJ booth tucked into the corner. A sliding wall is concealed in the booth; when Uta is done for the night, it rolls shut to hide and lock away the equipment.

### Sound

Speakers hang from ceiling-mounted poles and point in every direction, so sound reaches every corner of the room. It gets loud enough that people can feel the vibrations through their bodies.

Some people say it makes them get up and dance. Others say it is just really loud.

### Lighting

Mostly UV. Colored waves ripple across the floor, ceiling, and walls in response to the music. The entire room is washed in black light and neon, which is where the club gets its name.

### Entrance

The entrance sits at the front-right of the building. A ticket booth collects the cover charge. The entry door is controlled: guests can walk out freely, but cannot enter without the booth attendant pressing the button to unlock it.

## Admission

The official rule is 21+, but underage guests are welcome. Uta would rather have kids off the street than kicked out into it.

Minors have their hands stamped so the bar knows not to serve them alcohol.

Anyone who pays admission receives a wristband. The band acts as a key for restricted areas, including the games room door, and can optionally connect to an app for guests worried about losing the physical band. The app supports multiple active wristbands on one account, so regulars with overlapping passes do not have to juggle them.

### ID Verification

When someone buys a pass, staff scan their ID. A checksum of the ID data plus the pass’s expiration date is written to the wristband.

At the door, scanning the band pulls up the guest’s ID photo on the attendant’s screen. The attendant confirms that the face matches before allowing entry, making a wristband difficult to share.

### Glowing Emblems

Each wristband has a small emblem that glows under the club’s UV lighting. Only the emblem glows; the rest of the band stays dark so it does not distract from dancing.

The color tells staff which tier a guest has at a glance:

| Pass | Price | Per Day | Discount | Emblem |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---|
| Day | $20 | $20.00 | — | 🔴 Red |
| Week | $120 | $17.14 | 14% off | 🟢 Green |
| Month | $400 | $13.33 | 33% off | 🔵 Blue |
| Year | $3,500 | $9.59 | 52% off | 🟡 Yellow |
| Lifetime | $30,000 | ~$8.22 (10 years) | 59% off | ✨ Pearlescent |

The lifetime membership truly never expires. It is priced at roughly ten years of year passes—the house’s bet is that most people will not actually keep coming for that long.

The pearlescent emblem is the flex: it has to be extra fancy.

Passes expire at **10 AM**, well after closing. It is a funny detail, but the point is to guarantee every guest gets 100% of what they paid for.

## Bathrooms

The bathrooms are intentionally dim—not dark enough to be dangerous, but dim enough to discourage anyone trying to use them for drugs.

## Surveillance

Cameras cover the whole building. Because bass vibrations would shake a normal mount loose or blur the footage, every camera is mounted on springs to absorb the movement.

A dedicated staff member monitors the camera feeds. They have their own register for triggering the green all-staff alert through the order-disc system.

## Security

The ticket-booth attendant is armed. As soon as he leaves the booth, the whole building locks down.

Bouncers roam the rest of the club on foot, often passing themselves off as floor staff. One might look like he is sweeping up, but he is really just watching everyone.

They carry pepper spray or a taser—enough to handle drunks and fights without escalating. For anything more serious, hidden lockboxes throughout the building contain real weapons. Only staff who need to know are aware of their locations.

## Staff

Standard nightclub staff. Waiters take orders and carry them to either the bar or the kitchen, depending on what was ordered.

### Order Discs

Colorful pucks that vibrate when an order is ready—perfect for an atmosphere where verbal callouts cannot cut through the music.

When a waiter takes an order, they scan the disc and assign it to that order. Each register has its own color, and customers can also grab a disc themselves when ordering directly.

All staff carry one, including security. The disc lights up in color-coded categories:

| Color | Meaning | Source / Use |
|---|---|---|
| 🔴 Red | Food order | Kitchen |
| 🔵 Blue | Drink order | Bar |
| 🟢 Green | Security alert | Triggered by the camera watcher; when every staff disc vibrates green, it is fighting time |

## Location

On the edge of the city. You feel like the city is about to end, then it just keeps going a little further—and Aurora Pulse is right there.

## Hours

Roughly **8 PM to 4 AM**.

Every now and then, the club throws a special night: costume themes, dress-up nights, or whatever Uta feels like running. On those nights, the bouncers still work the floor in disguise.

## Games Room

The games room is on the right side of the building, through a set of doors. It is a quieter area under regular lighting, with a few pool tables, foosball, and other simple games.

A double-pane glass wall separates this space from the dance floor, reducing music bleed so competitive games are not drowned out by the bass.

## Kitchen & Bar

The kitchen and a bar-side dining area occupy the left side of the club under standard lighting. Clever engineering prevents the normal lights in this zone from affecting the black-light atmosphere of the dance floor.

## Back Hallway

Behind the kitchen and bar is a service hallway: the backbone of the staff area. It contains:

- Pantries for extra supplies
- A walk-in freezer for food
- Uta’s office
- The security office
- The staff room

The hallway exits from the back-left of the building through the staff door. Combined with the front-right main entrance, these are the only two ways in or out of the building.

Anyone going down this hallway should have business there. Otherwise, they get sent back to the dance floor quickly.

### Uta’s Office

Tucked into the back hallway next to the Security Office. Uta keeps the books, a safe for the night’s take, and anything else she does not want people touching. It is private and not open to patrons.

### Security Office

Across from Uta’s Office. The dedicated camera watcher sits here with access to every feed in the building. Its proximity to Uta’s office lets the two coordinate quickly when something goes sideways.

### Staff Room

A private staff-only room accessed from the back hallway. It includes a changing area and makeup station for staff going heavy on theme nights or dress-up shifts.

## VIP Booth

A private booth above the ticket booth, tucked into the front-right corner over the dance floor.

Its window is heavily tinted: no one outside can see in, and most patrons assume it is simply a regular window to the outside. From within, guests have a full view of the floor. It is ideal for regulars who need to stay invisible.

## Exterior

The entire building is painted in graffiti-style art: a sprawling professional mural rather than random tagging. It depicts the Aurora Borealis wrapping around the old warehouse shell, selling the club’s name before anyone reads the sign.

A few benches sit out front. There is no official patio, but people hang out there anyway. Uta likes giving younger kids a place to be off the street.

## Parking

A flat concrete slab with painted lines. No valet, no garage—just a place to park.

## Music

Sets build through the night:

| Time of Night | Atmosphere | Music Direction |
|---|---|---|
| Early evening | Relaxed; eases the crowd in | Chill vibes |
| As the room fills | Rising energy | Tempo gradually increases |
| Late night | Full hype; “going-off” music | House, techno, funk, and phonk |

Before the club opens and while it closes down, productivity radio plays over the speakers—quiet, unobtrusive background music for staff doing setup or cleanup.

## Menu

There are two menus:

| Menu | Audience | Description |
|---|---|---|
| Adult bar menu | Adults of drinking age | Full alcohol menu |
| Kids’ mocktail menu | Minors | Fancy non-alcoholic drinks served with the same glassware and presentation as alcoholic drinks |

The bar will pour elaborate mocktails for underage guests—same glassware, same presentation, no booze.

**Signature drink:** the **Shattered Cue Ball** mocktail, carried over from Uta’s earlier days.

## Dress Code

The rules:

- No weapons.
- No face coverings at the door. Faces must match the ID photo displayed during the wristband scan. Masks for theme nights are fine once guests are inside.
- Shoes required: spilled drinks, pool tables, and broken glass make bare feet a bad idea.
- Nothing blatantly offensive, including hate symbols or slurs. There are teens in the club.

There is no pat-down at the door. Uta does not have the manpower or the appetite for it. Bags are checked on entry, while the rest is handled on the honor system—with bouncers and the camera watcher as the fallback.

## Merch

A small glass display near the entrance holds Aurora Pulse merchandise. It is neither fancy nor aggressively promoted.

Anyone who wants something asks a staff member, who retrieves it from the back. The club website handles most merch sales anyway.

## Chimeran Policy

Uta knows Osyra is Chimeran. She does not ask questions, treat Osyra differently, or tell anyone else.

Osyra hides her features in public anyway, so most Aurora Pulse patrons would never notice.
