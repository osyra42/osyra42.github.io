# Blood Relation - Rev4 Outline

Talking-point reference. Per chapter, not per scene.

## I. Chapter 1 - The Hunting Lodge

- A. The lodge as cover: agency-dressed, taxidermy, antler chandelier, a costume that is also a building
- B. Her gear hides in plain sight - charge rifle in the rack, containment boxes as gun cases
- C. Home near dawn from a routine job; the vault box sits by the door, to be logged in the morning
- D. The real subject is the photograph on her nightstand, taken off a wall in a dead house months ago
- E. She took it without deciding to, which is not something she does
- F. Faded colors, ordinary ears, no red eyes - but the bones are her own face
- G. Decides overnight to take it to Vanity and stop pretending she doesn't need to know
- H. Korval arrives on time; photo goes in her coat, box in the back seat

## II. Chapter 2 - The Address

- A. Containment lounge; box logged through the slot and gone
- B. Vanity waiting, staged courtesy - she had Osyra on sensors since the parking level
- C. Slides a folder across: the woman in the photograph, identified from a face run
- D. Dr. Rosalyn Renaya, 68 - her own face forty years older
- E. Where the surname came from; it had never occurred to her it came from anywhere
- F. Details: kindergarten teacher, medical school in her late thirties, pediatrician, widowed, alone, two hours away
- G. The house with the photo was four miles from Rosalyn's school - a small county, not a coincidence
- H. "I want to go see her." "I assumed."
- I. Vanity's terms: real leave, Korval drives, agency calls ahead
- J. She hadn't made the call yet - the difference between a thing done to you and a thing you choose
- K. Parting instruction: wear the dress, the hat, the contacts - until you don't have to. And then take them off

## III. Chapter 3 - The Visit

- A. Nervousness she takes an hour to identify - no object, nothing to fight
- B. Korval drives; she asks how much further before deciding to, the second bad sign
- C. Changes in the back seat: dress, hat, contacts. Can't tell which version is real
- D. Ivy-covered house; Rosalyn walks out to meet them; Korval declines to come in and sits on the hood
- E. Osyra takes the seat by the door and knows exactly why, and doesn't move
- F. The chair, the light, the smell of newspaper and sunshine - it feels like a memory that isn't possible
- G. Cover story: military, combat unit, eliminating dangerous animals
- H. Rosalyn offers a Polaroid; Osyra says "Later" - declines to find out the thing she drove two hours to learn
- I. Chamomile, not coffee. Rosalyn doesn't announce it. Forty years of frightened children in her hands
- J. Photo album: the strict great-grandmother, then her mother on the porch steps holding a baby
- K. She recognizes the paint on the third step and nothing else - six years of a person and she kept a step
- L. Poverty named plainly: "a kind of poor that doesn't bend"
- M. Rosalyn's confession: they wouldn't let her near, she was being polite, and she let six years go
- N. Nobody in the foster process asked whether the child had anybody - the name was in the phone book
- O. The husband's photo: tracked nuisance animals; "anyone can pull a trigger, hard part is being sure you should"
- P. Rosalyn admits the quiet, the jars opened with a butter knife. Sends her to the back room

## IV. Chapter 4 - The Reveal

- A. Undresses in pieces: hat, dress, contacts. Looks in the mirror and looks like herself
- B. The arithmetic she hates - twelve hours out of the costume proves thirteen years had a bill on them
- C. Long shower; then the shake, water everywhere. Not a reflex she can't help - a thing she picks
- D. Borrowed nightshirt, too short. Opens the door and Rosalyn is standing there with a blanket
- E. She'd left it unlocked; she'd gotten comfortable in one afternoon and this is the cost
- F. Tries to make herself smaller, which doesn't work at six-four in a doorway
- G. Runs the familiar catalogue: the polite curtain, the phone call to the driver, the goodbye that means don't come back
- H. Rosalyn's shoulders come down. "Come into the light. Let me look at you properly."
- I. Osyra doesn't move; watches the hands, because that's where leaving shows first
- J. Rosalyn asks permission as a doctor - and the look on her face finally has a name: clinical worry, not revulsion
- K. Examines the ears: pain, pressure, fevers, skin trouble. "Oh, that's clever" - a word with no slot for it
- L. Then the anger, and Osyra reads it as disappointment before realizing it's pointed past her at the world
- M. "You don't ever do that in this house." Osyra checks the sentence twice for a hook and finds none
- N. "It was a lab. When I was sixteen." That's all she can do tonight. "Then that's all you need to do tonight."
- O. "I'd have loved you exactly like this. At sixteen. At six."

## V. Chapter 5 - The Letting Go

- A. Rosalyn sits down instead of leaving - a decision Osyra only later recognizes
- B. Small questions: ears under the hat, cold tail, normal food, the contacts like sand
- C. The bathroom floor joke - a joke about her body that isn't about whether she should exist
- D. The catalogue goes quiet. She hadn't known it could
- E. Spends the open window before it expires: Chimera 9, a shelf of numbered versions, the other kids
- F. Most didn't take; some came out worse, stopped looking like people. She doesn't know what happened to them
- G. The agency found her after the place came down; half the work is dangerous animals, half is recovery for rich people
- H. The real hurdle: can't go outside undisguised without someone getting scared or violent
- I. Eye exam under the lamp, saline advice, night vision
- J. The teeth - the thing she can't hide when she eats. Rosalyn checks the bite and finds it even
- K. "I'm glad you came. Even as you are."
- L. Alone in the dark she goes looking for the bracing and it isn't there - and nothing fell down
- M. If she was wrong about that, she's been wrong every day since she was six
- N. Checks in with Vanity: "You're a day early on sounding human."

## VI. Chapter 6 - The Morning

- A. Wakes before sunrise having slept hard, which she can't remember doing
- B. Finds the Polaroid on the counter and understands she'd misjudged which version Rosalyn meant
- C. First time her sorting instinct has been wrong in a way that cost her something
- D. Three photos for the record: straight-on, profile, teeth pulled back like a horse trader
- E. Then three for no reason: peace sign, blown kiss, full canine display
- F. Can't name what the last three were - there's a word for it and it's never applied to her
- G. Sticks the peace-sign one to the fridge under a pear magnet. It looks like a person, not an exhibit
- H. Slides the other five under the camera, face down, for Rosalyn to find
- I. Cooks: eggs, toast, cast iron, a full pot of coffee. First time making breakfast for someone in years
- J. Makes herself chamomile instead, four feet from real coffee, because it had been handed to her
- K. Rosalyn comes in, sees the fridge. "Well. Look at that." "You should do that more often."
- L. Finds the stack. Picks up the bared-teeth one first, puts it down last. "You look like the cover of a heavy metal album."
- M. Notices Osyra isn't drinking the coffee and lets it go without asking - from now on there will simply be chamomile
- N. Talks about after: a sandwich before any questions; training; learning to look human; "don't sniff things"

## VII. Chapter 7 - The Letter

- A. Osyra has rehearsed a clean professional thank-you for two hours
- B. The clean version dissolves. "Thank you for accepting me for who I am," and the last word cracks
- C. Rosalyn puts a hand on her shoulder. That's all it takes
- D. She breaks - reaches for the controls and finds them not connected to anything, for the first time as an adult
- E. Rosalyn thinks she's done something wrong; Osyra can't get her voice back to fix it, which makes it longer
- F. After: empty. Something carried, briefly not being carried
- G. Explains in the debrief voice: she's used to being told to leave by the third meeting, so she came in ready to
- H. Asks about her mother and father. Rosalyn: alive as far as she knows, a patch of land up north, a trailer, they grow what they eat
- I. Goes down the hall and opens a fifth door Osyra hadn't catalogued - the catalogue was already coming apart
- J. Returns with the last letter from her mother, a few years old, P.O. box return address
- K. The warning: they're proud, they won't take money or charity. Bring what you can't grow - salt, coffee, spices, vinegar
- L. "You take it. It belongs to you more than it belongs to me." Osyra holds it against her chest without planning to

## VIII. Chapter 8 - The Road North

- A. Korval arrives; they realize neither has the other's phone number and scramble
- B. Numbers traded on grocery-list paper; saved as ROSALYN R
- C. Rosalyn swats her shoulder as a scolding - no relative has ever done that, and she has no reaction ready
- D. "I'll come back when I can." "I know, honey." Hands raised from the porch as the house shrinks
- E. In the car she can't put the envelope in the bag; her hands won't do it
- F. Asks Korval how far north the car can go. "The driver, maybe, needs coffee."
- G. She could have gone home - the operation was complete. Going north wasn't the assignment
- H. No question prepared, and she has never walked into a room without knowing what she's there to get. Goes anyway
- I. Calls Vanity, extends leave to open-ended, same daily check-in
- J. Vanity already knows what Rosalyn advised: "Bring them something useful. Not money."
- K. Envelope goes in the jacket next to the 1985 photo - grandmother and the way to the rest of them, over her heart
- L. Takes the on-ramp the other way than home, and looks forward instead of bracing

## IX. Chapter 9 - The People Up North

- A. A day's drive; highway thins to a county road with grass in the seams
- B. Watches quiet rural poverty go by - different from city poor, struggling that stopped moving and put down roots
- C. Korval slows for a deer without a word
- D. Vanity's midday check-in: the P.O. box is still rented, renewed eight months ago, in cash
- E. Vanity's rule - she'll pull the thread that saves you a day, never the one that saves you the walk
- F. Hadley's Fork: feed store, diner, shed-sized post office, general store
- G. Shops in disguise: salt, whole and ground coffee, pepper, cinnamon, cardamom, cider vinegar, tea, matches, rope
- H. Nothing that looks like charity; everything that looks like someone thought about it
- I. Admits she's hiding in the arithmetic because it's the last thing between her and the road out
- J. The counterman asks if she's passing through. "Looking for family." "Most of it likes to be left alone."
- K. Four miles out on two ruts; the clearing opens
- L. Reads the site the way she reads any site and it gives back competence - every patch, splice, and repair a solved problem
- M. "I have been told my whole life that I came from nothing. I was looking at the nothing. It had a fence around it that had been repaired eleven times."
- N. Korval stops back from the trailer, engine running. "You are not going in as the soldier." "As the granddaughter, if that's a thing I get to be twice."
- O. Smoke off the stovepipe - somebody is home
- P. Rosalyn's rule doesn't apply here; this isn't her house, and Osyra doesn't know which version the door opens to
- Q. No plan, which two days ago she'd have called impossible
- R. Sets the bags down, hand goes to the hat brim and stops there. Doesn't decide. Knocks
