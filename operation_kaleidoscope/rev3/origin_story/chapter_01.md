# Chapter 01
## Operation Kaleidoscope / Chapter 01 / Childhood

### Present
After the announcement, I knew the media would be after me. We were public now. Journalists could come and go as they pleased, free to investigate and interrogate. I don't want the situation to be out of control. I set up a small site where people can submit their resumes. I chose exactly one journalist. There would be only one telling of the story. And that would be the end of it.

Today's the day the journalist I chose would begin the interview. She walks through the swarm on the streets to the door. I want to tell her the truth, to straighten out any misinformation that has been spread. She takes out a recorder, places it on my desk, and hits play before even looking at me.

She sits down and looks at me for a moment. I give her a light nod. I accept this. I wasn't hiding my form, I was wearing new branded official attire. She looks like she has heard everything. There is nothing new to tell her. Perfect. She wont't exaggerate the situation. Let's see how this goes. She shifts uncomfortably, but decides she must proceed.

She asks me how all of this started. My reply is as professional as I can make it. For journalism, you must be more specific. To which she says, alright, let's start with your childhood. 

I nod.

### Past
Alright, let's talk about my childhood. Let's see. I was born to extremely poor parents. We lived in the woods, beyond the road, in a place it felt like people should not even be. We lived in what remained of a trailer home, it had an add-on that seemed to try to swallow the entire trailer part. My dad built it so the family would have enough room to live. It didn't seem to matter how much he tried the house seemed to not want to be there, it would always try to fall apart. Dad tried to fight to keep it together.

Mom had a job as a maid cleaning other people's houses. She was too tired to keep ours completely clean, but she did her best. They did everything they could to get me proper materials to go to school. I loved school, it was a place to learn and I craved knowledge. My dad being able to fix anything motivated me to learn how to do the same. I needed to know everything.

Unfortunately, little by little we slowly slipped into extreme poverty. We struggled to make payments on the car. Dad tried to get work as a mechanic, but the way he learned to fix cars wasn't really by the book, and the boss didn't like it. Mom was still able to keep cleaning houses for the rich side of town, and that worked for a while. Dad was able to get some odd jobs, but they weren't very stable. Then slowly people whose homes Mom would clean started to move away and the new people didn't have a need for her. She was making less money and the cleaning business was drying up. People started to not have the extra money to pay Mom or Dad for their skills.

I was about 14 and just starting high school when my parents got a phone call, the system noticed just how poor we were and wanted nothing to do with it anymore. CPS came out and started asking me a ton of questions. Often they would ask the same thing over and over like I was going to change the answer. Where did I sleep? How long did my parents stay away from the house with me there alone? Did I feel safe? Did I have a cell phone? What did I eat yesterday? What kind of clothes did I have? What were my parents doing when I got home from school?

I didn't understand what was happening. I thought I was in trouble. I thought my parents were in trouble. As a teenager that thought I knew everything, in those moments I felt small as I answered. Everything felt wrong. I couldn't describe it. Was being poor a crime? They asked a lot of questions I didn't know how to answer. When they left and said they'd be in touch. I remember my Mom crying after they walked out. Dad just stood there with his hands in his pockets, staring at the floor.

A few weeks later a different group showed up. They looked official, maybe from the government or something. They said they could help. Said they had resources for kids, things like food, clothes, and education. They made it sound like my parents wouldn't have to worry anymore. My Mom didn't trust them. I could see it in her face. But my Dad was tired. We all were.

Their explanation said I could leave at any time, it was voluntary. But with piling debt and CPS snooping around, this all felt suspiciously convenient. After the lengthy explanation and pictures, they gave my parents papers to sign. I looked at them, any remaining hope of being able to pull out of the poverty sinkhole faded from their eyes. They could say no and fight the CPS, or give up here. They knew it was over. There were no more opportunities to escape. They signed.

The next morning a fancy car was outside waiting for me. A man I'd never seen before knocked on the door. He introduced himself as a chauffeur of the agency. He requested that I come to the door, where he explained that I could take a small backpack of personal things. Clothes, food, and other daily essentials would be provided. My Mom broke down, and my Dad choked up, I could hear it in his voice as he escorted me to my things. 

On the way out they asked the man when I would be back but his response was vague, something like, that's not how the program works and that I could return on my own as an adult. I didn't know it then but this man was from a secret government program, and was not an alternative to the CPS.

### Present
This journalist is exceptionally attentive. As I talk, she scribbles notes. She doesn't interrupt me and lets me continue my speech until I'm done. She knows this is a one-time opportunity. She must be precise and accurate with her interview. When she senses I have nothing else to say about my childhood, she asks her next question. So when did you find out the man was not a part of any of the government programs?
