# Chapter 01

After the announcement, I knew the media would be after me. We were public now. Journalists could come and go as they pleased, free to investigate and interrogate. One such journalist walked through the door. I wanted to tell her the truth, to straighten out any misinformation that had been spread.

She sat down and looked at me for a moment. I wasn't hiding my form, I was wearing new branded official attire. She looked like she was new to journalism and had to get the big scoop of the year. Let's see how this goes. She shifted uncomfortably, but decided she must proceed.

She asked me how all of this started. My reply was as professional as I could make it. For journalism, you must be more specific. To which she said, alright, let's start with your childhood. 

I nodded.

Let's start my childhood. I was born to extremely poor parents. I believe we lived in a trailer out in the woods. Parts of the house were almost never working. Dad tried to fight to keep it working. Mom had a job as a maid cleaning other people's houses. She was too tired to keep ours completely clean, but she did her best. They did everything they could to get me proper materials to go to school. I loved school, it was a place to learn and I craved knowledge. My dad being able to fix anything motivated me to learn how to do the same. I needed to know everything.

Unfortunately, little by little we slowly slipped into extreme poverty. We struggled to make payments on the car. dad tried to get work as a mechanic, but the way he learned to fix cars wasn't really by the book and the boss didn't like it. Mom was still able to keep cleaning houses for the rich side of town, and that worked for a while. dad was able to get some odd jobs, but they weren't very stable. Then slowly people who's homes mom would clean started to move away and the new people didn't have a need for her. She was making less money and the cleaning business was drying up. People started to not have the extra money to pay Mom or Dad for their skills.

I was about 16 when the school had enough of it and called CPS to see what was going on. They kept repeating the same questions. Where did I sleep? How long did my parents stay away from the house with me there alone? Did I feel safe? Did I have a cell phone? What did I eat yesterday? What kind of clothes did I have? What were my parents doing when I got home from school?

I didn't understand what was happening. I thought I was in trouble. I thought my parents were in trouble. As a teenager that thought I knew everything, in those moments I felt small as I answered. Everything felt wrong. I couldn't describe it. Was being poor a crime? They asked a lot of questions I didn't know how to answer.
